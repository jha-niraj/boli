# Ship the website answer desk first

Boli's next product should be the owner's leaning, kept narrow: **a paste-your-link "answer desk"**. A Nepali business enters its website (plus PDFs and a pasted FAQ). Boli crawls it and serves an embeddable text widget and an API that answer only from that content, in Nepali, Romanized Nepali and English, with source links. When the answer is not in the content, it says so and takes the visitor's number. It should be sold demo-led, first to **education and visa consultancies, clinics and hospitals, and real estate**. The evidence supports this on four counts. No Nepali self-serve "train it on my website" product with public NPR prices turned up. TingTing, the best-placed local rival, sells this only inside a sales-led inbox suite (Omni). Global tools start at $39-150 a month in dollars that Nepali SMEs struggle to pay. And the questions that flood Nepali inboxes ("price kati?", "delivery huncha?") are exactly what grounded answering handles. **The Tigg tax-and-law API ranks second: validate it now, build it next.** It is a different product: deterministic, CA-verified rate tables plus a cited "ask" endpoint, not chat. Today the signal is second-hand, and vidhica's corpus had only one finished Act in August 2026. The biggest technical risk sits under both products. Nobody has published a grounded-answer evaluation of Gemma 4 E4B, and small models tend to answer everything rather than refuse. So Boli must measure answer, refusal and script accuracy on real Nepali sites before the first demo, not after. Voice stays "later": TingTing already owns Nepali voice campaigns and phone agents.

## The slot is open because the incumbents look the other way

**TingTing**, Nepal's strongest AI communications company, is built around the phone call. It has six products: Call Broadcast, Text, Forms, Agents, Connect and Omni. Only **Omni**, launched April 2026, is text-first. It is a unified inbox for WhatsApp, Messenger, Instagram, email, TikTok and website chat, with AI replies trained on PDFs, web pages and Google Docs ([Omni page](https://www.tingting.io/product/tingting-omni)). Omni publishes no languages, no prices and no customers. TingTing sells its AI-conversational products through sales ("For TingTing Agents and TingTing Connect, please contact our team directly for pricing") ([TingTing FAQs](https://www.tingting.io/faqs)). Its developer page lists only campaign, OTP and report endpoints, and some sections appear to be copied from Modal's docs ([For developers](https://www.tingting.io/for-developers)). TingTing's real strength is enterprise voice: **50+ B2B clients and 10M interactions** claimed ([tingting.io](https://www.tingting.io)), named customers WorldLink, Vianet, Citizens Bank, eSewa, CellPay and Deprosc Laghubitta ([ICT Samachar, Feb 2026](https://ictsamachar.com/news/tingting-a-nepali-ai-startup-unifying-business-communication-on-a-single-platform/)), and a Sri Lanka deal with Dialog Axiata ([Headline Nepal, Aug 2026](https://english.headlinenepal.com/details/48055)). Boli should not fight it on voice. It should take the long tail that an enterprise sales team skips: schools, clinics, consultancies, shops and cooperatives that want a self-serve answer box at a published price.

The global "URL to chatbot" category shows what the product is and what it costs. Chatbase, SiteGPT, DocsBot and CustomGPT all sell the same bundle: crawl or upload, a widget, Messenger and WhatsApp connectors, an API, and paid branding removal. Entry prices are **$150/mo at Chatbase** ([Chatbase pricing](https://www.chatbase.co/pricing)), **$59/mo at SiteGPT** with manual refresh only ([SiteGPT pricing](https://sitegpt.ai/pricing)), **$49/mo at DocsBot**, which also has a free tier of 50 pages ([DocsBot pricing](https://docsbot.ai/pricing)), and **$99/mo at CustomGPT** ([CustomGPT pricing](https://customgpt.ai/pricing/)). They gate re-crawl frequency (manual, monthly, weekly, daily) and branding removal as upsells, and Boli can copy both levers. Reviews complain about cost predictability, not answer quality. Chatbase reviewers call messages "a bit expensive" and report vanishing training data ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10012414/Chatbase/reviews/)). Intercom Fin users dislike paying $0.99 for "assumed" resolutions ([G2 Fin reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/fin-by-intercom/reviews)). Prepaid NPR credits and a visible "what we trained on" page answer both complaints. **Sarvam** does not occupy the slot either. Its conversational product is a per-minute voice-agent platform (₹3.00-3.50/min) with no website widget or crawl ([Sarvam voice pricing](https://docs.sarvam.ai/voice-agents/pricing)).

The closest local threat is not TingTing but **Diyo AI's Agent Studio**. It is a self-serve, no-code website chat-agent builder with Google sign-up, "No credit card" and "Get started free". It has no API key, docs or pricing, and its research did not establish whether it crawls a URL ([Diyo Agent Studio](https://agentstudio.diyo.ai/)). Boli must try it hands-on before the first demo. Agencies fill the rest of the market. Sajedar sells a Messenger AI bot at **NPR 2,500-12,000/month** ([Sajedar / DEV](https://dev.to/dimanjan/architecting-a-2-second-facebook-messenger-ai-chatbot-for-nepal-e-commerce-486)). Praxium quotes custom builds at **NPR 150,000-300,000 plus NPR 8,000-20,000/month** ([Praxium Labs](https://praxiumlabs.com/blog/ecommerce-chatbot-roi-nepal/)). Nepdev is the only vendor found that names Romanized Nepali ([AI Agents Directory](https://aiagentsdirectory.com/agency/nepdev-technology)). Pathao Commerce bundles a Facebook, Instagram and TikTok inbox for a 2% commission and signed 600+ merchants in two weeks ([Kathmandu Post, 2026-08-27](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2026/08/27/fast-delivery-changes-the-game-for-nepal-s-online-sellers)). No one offers what Boli would: self-serve setup in minutes, published NPR prices, eSewa or Khalti payment, open models, and Romanized Nepali as a tested feature.

## Nepali businesses drown in the same ten questions, typed in Roman script

The pain is consistent across sources, though almost all of it is vendor-reported, because no independent owner survey exists. Real inbox messages look like "kati parcha", "delivery charge kasto cha" and "L size cha ki chaina" ([Sajedar / DEV](https://dev.to/dimanjan/architecting-a-2-second-facebook-messenger-ai-chatbot-for-nepal-e-commerce-486)). Sellers describe "answering the same questions all day", and note that "replies slow down when you are busy or asleep" and "customers who wait buy elsewhere" ([Blanxer](https://www.blanxer.com/sell-on-instagram-facebook-nepal)). A support agent in Kathmandu costs about **NPR 40,000-60,000/month** all-in. Realistic bot deflection in Nepal is **35-60% blended**: order status 85-95%, product information 60-75%, complaints 5-15% ([Praxium Labs](https://praxiumlabs.com/blog/ecommerce-chatbot-roi-nepal/)). One unnamed Kathmandu consultancy fielded 300+ messages a day ([EasyGrow](https://www.easygrowsolution.com/blog/ai-chatbot-nepal)). Romanized input is where South Asian bots break. Haptik found that missing Hinglish support caused **40-80% of bot breaks** for two customers ([Haptik blog](https://www.haptik.ai/blog/multilingual-chatbots-hinglish)). That is the best public proxy for what Romanized Nepali will do to a keyword bot.

Two findings shape the product. First, **the conversations mostly happen on Meta, not on websites.** Nepal had 15.0 million social media identities in October 2026 ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2027-nepal)). A vendor claims over 85% of social-commerce transactions happen in Messenger and Instagram DMs ([Sajedar / DEV](https://dev.to/dimanjan/architecting-a-2-second-facebook-messenger-ai-chatbot-for-nepal-e-commerce-486)). Viber jumped to the most-downloaded app during the September 2025 social media block ([Nepali Telecom](https://www.nepalitelecom.com/2025/09/viber-becomes-most-download-app-in-nepal-after-social-media-ban.html)). A website widget alone therefore suits businesses that *have* content-rich websites and leads that land on them: consultancies, colleges, clinics, hospitals and real estate. It does not suit Instagram shops. They come later, with the Messenger channel. Second, **the money is in NPR and small.** Debit cards are capped at Rs 20,000 per transaction ([Gadgetbyte](https://www.gadgetbytenepal.com/nepal-rastra-bank-transaction-limits/)). Dollar-card limits push businesses toward informal channels ([ICT Frame](https://ictframe.com/?p=113199)). Local SaaS anchors sit at a free tier plus **NPR 999/month** ([Bathuwa](https://bathuwa.com/)). Tigg shows that Nepali SMEs accept annual prepayment, selling 1-, 3- and 10-year plans paid in advance ([Tigg pricing](https://tiggapp.com/pricing)). Whether eSewa or Khalti support true auto-renewing billing is unverified. Until it is confirmed, use prepaid monthly packs and annual plans.

## A 4B open model can do this only with guard rails built around it

The stack fits what Boli already runs, but the model is the weak link. On the positive side, **Cloudflare Browser Rendering's `/crawl`** renders JavaScript, returns Markdown, follows sitemaps and respects robots.txt by default. It runs as an async job, which fits a Cloudflare Workflow ([Cloudflare changelog](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/2026-03-10-br-crawl-endpoint); [robots.txt reference](https://developers.cloudflare.com/browser-rendering/reference/robots-txt)). **BGE-M3** (MIT) is the only embedder with Nepali evidence, at 84.0% Recall@1 in a Nepali benchmark ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/408986977_How_Well_Does_AI_Understand_Nepali_A_Multi-Task_Benchmark_of_Large_Language_Models_and_Embedding_Models_on_a_Low-Resource_Language)). **bge-reranker-v2-m3** runs in `llama-server` with `--reranking` ([llama.cpp rerank guide](https://www.simplified.guide/_export/xhtml/llama-cpp/server-call-rerank-api)). **pgvector on Neon** handles 1024-dimension vectors with HNSW ([Neon pgvector](https://neon.com/docs/extensions/pgvector)), which keeps chunks in the same tenant-filtered database. **IndicXlit** handles Roman-to-Devanagari Nepali at about 11M parameters ([IndicXlit](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AI4Bharat/IndicXlit/master/README.md)). The repo's docintel work already detects and converts Preeti, the legacy Nepali font encoding that silently corrupts extracted text.

On the negative side, **no one has published a RAG-faithfulness evaluation for Gemma 4 E4B.** Research shows small instruction-light models "answer every question and ignore refusal instructions", and prompting alone does not fix that ([arXiv 2608.27661](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27661)). In a comparable low-resource Indic language, baseline models scored about **20-40% citation F1 and 0% refusal accuracy** before fine-tuning ([AssameseLM, arXiv 2611.06170](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06170)). E4B also scores only **25.4% on the MRCR v2 128K long-context test** ([Gemma 4 model card](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/model_card_4)), so contexts must stay short: the top 5 reranked chunks, roughly 3-6K tokens. Romanized queries against Devanagari pages are the weakest retrieval path. A community Nepali embedder rates its own Romanized support as "partial" ([nepali-e5-base-v1](https://huggingface.co/Ganu000/nepali-e5-base-v1)). The design answer is to put the trust in code rather than in the prompt. If the best rerank score falls below a threshold, skip generation and return a fixed "not on our site, leave your number" reply. Strip citations that point at sources that do not exist. Run retrieval twice for Romanized queries, once as typed and once transliterated, and fuse the results. Check that the reply is in the user's script. Later, a LoRA on grounded-answer and refusal examples in all three scripts is the documented route to production-grade refusal ([OCC-RAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00683)). It also fits Boli's fine-tune roadmap. **Gemma 4 26B-A4B** (about 4B active parameters) is the step-up model for paid tiers or hard questions ([Gemma 4 model card](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/model_card_4)).

The economics favour value pricing. At Boli's own published rate for boli-chat (Rs 2 input and Rs 6 output per million tokens, in `packages/boli/src/boli.ts`), a turn with about 4K tokens of prompt and 250 of output costs about **Rs 0.01**. A four-turn conversation costs about **Rs 0.04**, against the NPR 1.5-3.5 per conversation that Praxium budgets for commercial LLM APIs ([Praxium Labs](https://praxiumlabs.com/blog/ecommerce-chatbot-roi-nepal/)). E4B decodes at about **130 tokens/s on an NVIDIA L4** ([AWS builder guide](https://builder.aws.com/content/3LHTWAMUeCaDq0EB6JxSfqXRCGe/size-your-model-to-where-you-run-it-gemma-4-e2b-and-e4b-on-an-aws-l4-and-two-small-gpus)), so one GPU serves thousands of chats a day. The real cost is the always-on GPU, not tokens. Boli is selling trust (citations, refusals, Romanized Nepali), not compute. Those are inferences: GPU rental prices and concurrent-slot throughput were not measured.

| 4 | **Messenger, then Viber and WhatsApp channels** for the answer desk | After v1 has real customers | That is where shops talk. **Tigg is a real prize and should not be ignored.** It claims **10,000+ users across 1,500 businesses** ([tiggapp.com](https://tiggapp.com)). Its CEO is a chartered accountant ([Kathmandu Post, 2022](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2022/06/27/tigg-cloud-based-accounting-software-making-strides)). In October 2026 it signed an MoU with ICAN that gives qualifying CA firms free lifetime Basic access ([Beema Post](https://en.beemapost.com/2026/10/14752/)), which puts it in front of the people who ask tax-rate questions. The questions are hard because the answers depend on conditions. Service-fee TDS is **1.5% to a VAT-registered provider and 15% otherwise**. Contract payments over Rs 50,000 aggregate over 10 days. Rent to an entity can carry withholding at both IRD and the local level ([Baker Tilly Tax Fact 2025/26](https://bakertilly.com.np/storage/download/1750310698_Tax_Fact_2025-2026.pdf)). FY 2083/84 reportedly doubled the 1% band to Rs 10 lakh, cut the top rate from 39% to 29% and added 5% VAT on electricity above 50 units ([Lawalpine](https://lawalpine.com/blog/budget-2083-84-tax-changes-in-nepal)). IRD then issued new directives into Mangsir 2083 ([IRD](https://ird.gov.np)). Secondary sources disagree on several of these numbers, and even Baker Tilly's rate card mislabels BS dates. A ledger system already knows the payee's VAT status, the amount and the date, so the valuable product is a **structured call from inside Tigg**, not a chat box. Avalara is the template. It promises 99.99% uptime in its marketing and caps an accuracy guarantee at the annual fee ([Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/us/en/products/calculations.html); [Avalara 2015](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2015/10/avalara-announces-first-of-its-kind-sales-tax-accuracy-guarantee.html)).

The steelman for doing Tigg first is strong. One named buyer with 1,500 businesses behind it beats a hypothesis about 60 SMEs. Three facts still keep it second. The demand is a sentence relayed through the owner's brother, with no format, volume or budget attached. The engine is mostly not an LLM: the number path must be **curated, CA-approved rate rows served by deterministic code, with no model in it**, which is data and legal work more than model work. And vidhica's corpus, per its own `docs/nucleus-bilingual-headings.md`, had **only the Income Tax Act 2058 READY (255 chunks)** as of 2026-08-07, built on OpenAI and Sarvam models that Boli would have to replace with open ones. The right move is a cheap discovery now: one meeting, a written spec, and a request for a paid-pilot letter. If Tigg commits, build a narrow FY 2083/84 slice (TDS table, payroll slabs, BS/AD deadlines, late interest) with the brother as CA reviewer. That slice is a 4-6 week job that runs alongside the answer desk without competing for the GPU. The two products also share an ingestion engine (Preeti fix, open embeddings, grounded and cited answers), so every week on the answer desk makes the later `ask` endpoint cheaper.

## v1 scope, NPR prices and a 60-business demo test

**v1 scope (about four to six weeks).** v1 has eight parts:

- **Ingestion.** URL submit with an "I own or manage this site" check. A `/crawl` Workflow capped at about 200 pages, plus PDF upload and a pasted FAQ or price list for businesses that live on Facebook.
- **Cleanup and indexing.** Preeti detection and conversion. Chunks of 300-500 tokens carrying heading path and URL. BGE-M3 embeddings in pgvector plus Postgres full-text and trigram search, fused, then reranked.
- **Romanized queries.** IndicXlit normalisation of the query.
- **Answering.** E4B answers with numbered source links, behind a score-threshold refusal that falls back to lead capture ("leave your number").
- **Manual overrides.** Question-and-answer pairs the owner writes, which beat retrieval for prices and fees.
- **Widget.** A script-tag widget with colour, logo, greeting and starter questions in three scripts. Security comes from a public key, a domain allowlist, rate limits and streaming.
- **Owner dashboard.** Pages ingested, manual re-crawl, transcripts, unanswered questions and captured leads.
- **API.** The same bot reachable through the existing `/v1` API with a bot id.

Out of v1: messaging channels, a live human-handoff inbox (v1 hands off by email or phone link), scheduled re-crawl, the 26B model and voice.

**Pricing hypothesis.** These are proposals anchored on the market figures above, to be tested in the pilots:

| Plan | NPR | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| Free | 0 | 1 bot, 50 pages, 100 answers/month, "Powered by Boli" |
| Starter | **Rs 1,499/month** (or Rs 14,999/year) | 1 bot, 300 pages, 1,500 answers, manual re-crawl, lead capture |
| Business | **Rs 4,999/month** (or Rs 49,999/year) | 3 bots, 2,000 pages, 6,000 answers, weekly re-crawl, branding removed, lead export |
| Institution | from **Rs 14,999/month** | Multiple bots, the bigger model, Messenger when ready, priority support |
| Setup service | Rs 5,000-15,000 one-time | Boli builds, tunes and installs the bot |

Extra answers come out of the existing NPR credit wallet at about Rs 1 per answer. API-only developers pay the existing per-token rates. Starter sits between Bathuwa's Rs 999 and Sajedar's Rs 2,500 floor. Business sits inside the NPR 5,000-15,000 range that consultancies and clinics can bear. Both are far below agency builds. Price per *answer*, not per conversation or per "resolution", so the bill stays predictable.

**First target sectors, in order.** First, **education and visa consultancies and private colleges**: content-heavy sites, high value per lead, ad-driven evening traffic, and a cited 300+ messages a day ([EasyGrow](https://www.easygrowsolution.com/blog/ai-chatbot-nepal)). Second, **clinics, hospitals and diagnostic labs**: fees, timings, doctors' schedules and after-hours questions. Third, **real estate**, where ad leads go cold ([EasyGrow](https://www.easygrowsolution.com/blog/ai-chatbot-nepal)). Hotels and trekking agencies are an untested fourth guess: they have English and Nepali sites and foreign visitors. Social-commerce shops wait for the Messenger channel. Banks and ISPs run weak, rule-based bots ([Swift Technology](https://www.swifttech.com.np/blog/nepali-banks-digital-chat-assistants/)) but buy on long, compliance-heavy cycles, so they come later.

**Validation plan.** The plan has four stages.

- **Weeks 0-2: build an eval set first.** Take five real Nepali business websites. Write 100-200 questions per site, across Devanagari, Romanized and English, including unanswerable questions drawn from the same content ([arXiv 2412.12300](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.12300)). Do not show a customer until the bot passes proposed gates: at least 85% correct on answerable questions, at least 90% correct refusals, at least 95% replies in the right script, and zero invented prices.
- **Weeks 2-5: run the "reverse demo".** Agencies already build a bot from a prospect's site and send the link before the first call ([Ciela](https://ciela.ai/blogs/reverse-demo-method-ai-agencies)). Its conversion claims are unproven ([GrowwStacks](https://growwstacks.com/blog/how-to-sell-ai-agents-with-personalized-demos)). List 60 prospects, 20 per sector, in Kathmandu Valley. Build each bot from public pages only, behind a private, noindexed link. Send it with one line: "ask it in Romanized Nepali". Follow up in person, since a phone in the owner's hand is the demo. Never put it on their site without consent.
- **Weeks 5-9: free 30-day pilots, then a paid ask.** Install free 30-day pilots on the sites that say yes. Review the unanswered-question log weekly with each owner, and ask for payment at day 30. Measure seven numbers: demo-link opens, meetings, installs, real visitor questions, the answered rate, the share of wrong answers found in owner review, and leads captured.
- **Week 9: decide.** Proposed success line: at least 10 installs from 60 prospects, and at least 5 paying at Starter or above by week 9. Under 3 installs means the pitch or the sectors are wrong. Installs that do not convert mean the price or the value is wrong. Many requests for Messenger mean that channel moves into v1.5.

Run the Tigg track in parallel. In week 1, meet Tigg through the brother. Ask what surfaces they want (voucher entry, an ask box, a statutory reports explainer), expected call volume, the payment model (per call, flat, or revenue share), data-residency needs and acceptable liability terms. The goal is a signed paid-pilot letter before any corpus work starts.

## The risks are trust, channel fit and a free competitor

Five risks stand out:

- **Answer quality** is the first. One confidently wrong fee or price on a clinic's site ends the account and the referral. That is why the eval gates, the refusal threshold and the manual overrides come before sales.
- **Channel fit** is the second. If most target businesses get their questions in Messenger rather than on their website, the widget answers the wrong door, and Messenger has to move up the roadmap.
- **Competition** is the third. Diyo's free Agent Studio, TingTing Omni with its enterprise logos, Pathao's inbox and cheap agencies could each cap the price. TingTing could also expose an Omni API quickly.
- **Fixed cost** is the fourth. An always-on GPU costs the same with five customers as with five hundred, and a public widget invites abuse that burns it. The answer is per-bot rate limits and a monthly answer cap tied to credits.
- **Founder bandwidth** is the fifth. Demo-led selling across 60 businesses plus a Tigg discovery is a lot for one person, and the in-person demos are the step most likely to slip.

The Tigg product has its own risks:

- A wrong rate flowing silently into thousands of vouchers.
- A missed mid-year directive.
- Conflicting secondary sources.
- No Nepali precedent found on a software vendor's liability for a wrong tax figure. Legal advice is needed before signing.

## Open questions the owner must answer

These decisions change the work, so they need the owner's call before building:

1. **Crawling.** Does "only our own open models" allow Cloudflare's `/crawl` service for crawling, or must crawling be self-hosted (Crawl4AI, Apache-2.0)? `packages/webdata` currently wraps hosted Exa and Firecrawl, and Firecrawl's self-host licence is AGPL-3.0 ([firecrawl GitHub](https://github.com/firecrawl/firecrawl)).
2. **PDF OCR.** Is Sarvam's document OCR acceptable for PDFs, or must Boli use its own docintel routes?
3. **Hosting and residency.** Where does the GPU run? Tigg's CEO cited a rule that servers must be in Nepal ([Kathmandu Post, 2022](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2022/06/27/tigg-cloud-based-accounting-software-making-strides)), and a Nepal cloud such as CoreValley may matter for both products.
4. **vidhica data.** Can Boli reuse vidhica's Nucleus data and pipeline, and on what terms?
5. **Demos.** Who runs the in-person demos, and is direct selling to SMEs a motion Boli wants to own, or should agencies resell under a partner plan?
6. **Free tier.** Should there be a free tier at all, given GPU cost and abuse risk?
7. **Product identity.** Does the answer desk carry the Boli name, or get its own product name for non-developer buyers?
8. **Tigg liability.** What accuracy commitment and liability cap will Boli accept from Tigg, and who signs off on each rate row?

## Conclusion

The two opportunities look like a choice but are one roadmap. Both depend on the same hard, Nepal-specific skills: reading broken Preeti PDFs, matching Romanized questions to Devanagari text, and getting a small open model to say "I don't know" in the user's own script. The answer desk is the cheaper place to learn them, because a wrong answer costs one SME account rather than an audit finding. Each pilot also yields evaluation data that later hardens the tax `ask` endpoint. The tax API's money path, by contrast, should never touch a model. That is a useful discipline for Boli as a whole: use open models where language is the problem, and use plain tables where numbers are.

The research also changes what Boli is selling. At its own token price, a whole conversation costs about four paisa, so compute is not the product. The product is a Nepali business trusting a machine to speak for it in Romanized Nepali at 10 PM. That trust is earned on measured refusal rates and shown sources, and a demo link built from the owner's own website is the fastest way to find out whether anyone will pay for it.
