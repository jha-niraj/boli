# Nepal still lacks its own Sarvam

As of 23 November 2026, **no Nepal-based company runs a Sarvam-style platform**. None offers instant signup, free credits, a model playground, an API key, public docs and published per-unit prices for raw Nepali speech-to-text, text-to-speech, translation or LLM chat. We scored nineteen Nepal candidates on a 14-point Sarvam checklist. The best is **TingTing (Prixa) at 5 of 14**, against roughly 13 for Sarvam's own platform. TingTing gets there through self-serve signup with 100 free credits and a public API reference. But that API runs phone campaigns, not raw speech models. The next tier scores 2 to 2.5 each, and each covers a different slice. **Koili.ai** has a self-serve Nepali TTS account with an "API key" step but no docs or prices. **HimalayaGPT on ScaLabs Cloud** is an OpenAI-compatible Nepali LLM endpoint, but keys are issued by hand. **Ampixa Labs** and **Naamche Labs** have the strongest open Nepali speech models and free playgrounds, but no hosted API. Nothing new and Sarvam-like launched in October or November 2026. The only movement was in open weights (Ampixa's Rani and Ratna ASR, Naamche's Swar), a waitlisted voice API (Awaj AI), and invite-only local GPU clouds (CoreValley, YetiCloud). For Boli, the slot is empty. Its strongest openings are Nepali TTS, which even Sarvam does not offer, a hosted transliteration API that nobody in Nepal sells, and NPR billing on Nepal-standard (`ne-NP`) models. The main risks are the labs adding hosted APIs and the Bhashini x Kathmandu University platform, which has not shipped.

## What "Sarvam-like" means, and how little of it Sarvam gives Nepali

Sarvam sets the bar for developer onboarding. Signup gives **Rs 100 of free credits that never expire**. One credit wallet covers every API. Prices are published in INR per unit across a pay-as-you-go tier and four plans, from Rs 9,999 to Rs 1,99,999 a month. Rate-limit numbers are not published ([Sarvam API pricing](https://www.sarvam.ai/api-pricing)). You create keys in a dashboard, and official SDKs exist for Python (`sarvamai`) and JS/TS. The docs come with an MCP server, llms.txt and a dated changelog ([Sarvam quickstart](https://docs.sarvam.ai/api-reference-docs/getting-started/quickstart); [Sarvam changelog](https://docs.sarvam.ai/changelog/2026/8/18)). A startup programme gives 6-12 months of credits in exchange for a "Powered by Sarvam" badge ([Sarvam Startup Program](https://www.sarvam.ai/startup-program)). From these features we built a 14-point checklist:

1. Instant self-serve key
2. Free credits
3. Public local-currency unit prices
4. Playground
5. Public docs
6. SDKs
7. STT with streaming
8. TTS with voices and cloning
9. Translation and transliteration
10. Native LLM
11. Document AI
12. Higher-level products (agents, dubbing)
13. Startup programme
14. Published rate limits and SLA tiers

Each item scores 1 (present), 0.5 (partial or claimed but unverified) or 0.

Sarvam's Nepali coverage is much thinner than its platform. Nepali is listed as **`ne-IN` (Indian Nepali)**. It works only in **Saaras v4 STT**, **Sarvam-Translate** and **Sarvam Vision OCR**. It is **not supported in Bulbul TTS (v3 or v4 Flash), in Mayura translation, or in the Sarvam-105B LLM** ([Sarvam models](https://docs.sarvam.ai/api-reference-docs/getting-started/models)). Our October dossier treated Saaras v3 as Sarvam's Nepali STT model. Saaras v4 is now the default, and v3 is still available. Sarvam's Nepali STT costs **Rs 0.49 per minute** on pay-as-you-go. That is about NPR 0.78 at the fixed 1.6 INR-NPR peg, a conversion we assumed rather than sourced ([Sarvam API pricing](https://www.sarvam.ai/api-pricing)). So a developer building in Nepali on Sarvam today gets a polished platform with a **missing voice and a missing brain**.

**TingTing** comes closest on onboarding. Its homepage offers self-serve signup with **"100 free AI credits... no card required"**, plus an Experience Center, "Call Me Now" and a sample of its "Ashmita" voice ([tingting.io](https://www.tingting.io)). Credits are metered by call time: one voice credit covers 30 seconds, a survey costs 3 credits per call and an AI inbound call costs 5. No price per credit is published in NPR, and the FAQ contradicts itself on how outbound calls are billed ([TingTing FAQs](https://www.tingting.io/faqs)). The developer page lists endpoints for auth, API tokens, campaigns, a "test voice" call and OTP. It has **no raw TTS or STT endpoint**, and some of its sections appear to be copied from Modal's site ([For developers](https://www.tingting.io/for-developers)). The new **TingTing Omni** is an omnichannel inbox with AI replies, not an API ([TingTing Omni](https://www.tingting.io/product/tingting-omni)). In October we recorded five Nepali voices. The site now names only Ashmita, so that count is unconfirmed. TingTing is Sarvam's onboarding wrapped around a call-centre product. Nepali TTS is reachable only indirectly, inside campaign calls billed by call time.

**Koili.ai** is the closest in shape to a raw TTS API, and the least finished. Its signup form is live, and the homepage promises a "Generate API access key" step. But the Features, API, Demo and Pricing links all point to "#", and the footer still reads © 2025 ([koili.ai](https://koili.ai); [Koili signup](https://koili.ai/accounts/signup/)). Nothing on the site changed between October and November, which suggests the product has stalled. **Diyo AI** now lists Translation, TTS, STT and Subtitling as "Language Services". It names an API, Documentation and SDKs, but the footer entries for docs and SDKs have no URLs, and every call to action is "Talk to Diyo" ([diyo.ai](https://www.diyo.ai)). Its self-serve **Agent Studio** offers Google sign-up with no card. It is a no-code website chat-agent builder, not a model API ([Diyo Agent Studio](https://agentstudio.diyo.ai/)).

**TalkC.ai** is the only Nepal company that publishes cash prices. It charges **$0.03-0.06 per minute** for inbound and answered outbound calls, plus a monthly platform fee and setup fees quoted per business ([TalkC pricing](https://talkc.ai/pricing)). Its "free trial" is a form, and the team calls you back within two hours ([talkc.ai](https://talkc.ai)). That per-minute price covers a whole voice-agent call (STT, LLM, TTS and telephony), so it cannot be compared with API unit prices. **WiseAI** has added a "Try Now" link to a registration app. The parent site claims native Nepali and Maithili ASR and TTS inside a SIP voice-agent product ([WiseAI product page](https://www.wiseyak.com/products/wiseai/)). We did not open the app, so we do not know what an account gets you. **Paaila** has effectively left software. Its domain now serves a Wayback Machine Downloader copy with roughly 2017 content ([paailatechnology.com](https://paailatechnology.com)). One research pass still saw the old "API coming real soon" line on its TTS page ([Paaila](https://paailatechnology.com/ai/nepali-text-to-speech)). A direct read the same day found only the downloader's demo notice. Either way, there is no API.

## The best Nepali models sit in open labs with no API key

The biggest quality jump since October came from two small labs that publish models instead of selling them. **Ampixa** lists 61 projects. Its new releases include **Rani**, an open-weights ASR model for offline and streaming use, and **Ratna**, a 35 MB ASR model that runs in the browser. It also released the **Eka** voice cloner (12 November) and the **Yak** tokenizer, alongside Kala TTS, sanoTTS and a Devanagari OCR. There is no API, no pricing and no general signup ([ampixa.com](https://ampixa.com)). On Hugging Face, the real-nepali-tts Space is running and the gurung-tts Space is erroring ([HF API ampixa spaces](https://huggingface.co/api/spaces?author=ampixa&limit=100)). The **NepaliConformer** models we recommended in October are **gone from Ampixa's 25 public models**, which matches the 401 errors we hit in October ([HF API ampixa models](https://huggingface.co/api/models?author=ampixa&limit=100)). **Naamche** has added a public **Swar Playground**: 9 ready-made voices, about 30-second voice cloning, and Nepali, English or mixed input, with "nothing to install". Its other models are **Kriti V1** ASR (24.08% WER), **Kriti Telephony** (32.38 WER on NepTel) and **BarunLM 35M**, and the only call to action is an email link ([naamchelabs.com](https://naamchelabs.com); [Naamche Playground](https://naamchelabs.com/playground)).

These labs come closest to Sarvam's *models*, but they have no platform. Their licences limit how a commercial host can reuse the models. **Swar** and **Kala** are CC-BY-SA-4.0, **sanoTTS** is GPL-3.0, **Rani** and **Ratna** carry an unread "other" licence, and **Kriti** is MIT ([HF Aarjan/swar](https://huggingface.co/Aarjan/swar); [HF sanoTTS](https://huggingface.co/ampixa/sanoTTS); [HF rani-asr](https://huggingface.co/ampixa/rani-asr); [Kriti V1 page](https://naamchelabs.com/models/kriti-v1)). Either lab could add a hosted endpoint with little effort. That makes them both Boli's best upstream suppliers and its most capable potential competitors.

For LLM chat, the only hosted Nepal-built option is **HimalayaGPT 0.5B**. It was trained with a custom Nepali tokenizer and is served from Kathmandu hardware by ScaLabs Cloud, which says it is "the host, not the author". The endpoint is OpenAI-compatible, but access means filling in a form and waiting up to a working day for a key. The same page calls the service both "Free - fair-use rate-limited" and "Pay-per-use" ([ScaLabs Cloud HimalayaGPT page](https://scalabs-cloud.pages.dev/inference/himalayagpt-0-5b)). Himalaya AI Labs says it is building HimalayaGPT as a "sovereign Nepali LLM" for the Nagarik App and Hello Sarkar. We found no government confirmation ([HimalayaAI on X](https://x.com/HimalayaAILabs/status/2041206703153402135)). Among open models, **Arkios 1B** stands out. It is the only Nepali generative model we found with a clearly permissive licence (Apache-2.0) and published evaluations, scoring 81% correct-script output on zero-shot Nepali-to-English translation ([HF arkios-1b-chat](https://huggingface.co/sajalregmi4/arkios-1b-chat)). Every Nepal-built LLM is between 0.5B and 2B parameters, far below the frontier models that already handle Nepali. **GuffGPT** and **Hajur AI** are consumer chat apps with no API ([guffgpt.com](https://guffgpt.com/); [Janakhabar](https://janakhabar.com/news-and-technology/2026/03/07/119339/)).

Translation and transliteration are almost entirely missing from Nepal's market. **AI4Nepal** was founded in 2024 by IIT Madras alumni. It advertises a translation API, ASR, TTS and an NLP toolkit, all reached through a contact page ([aifornepal.com.np](https://www.aifornepal.com.np/)). We found **no Nepal company with a hosted Romanized-to-Devanagari transliteration API**. The only options are open-source libraries such as `nepali_unicoder` ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/nepali_unicoder/)).

## New entrants since October are clouds, waitlists and memoranda

None of the 2026 newcomers changes the answer. **CoreValley AI** calls itself "Nepal's own AI cloud". It runs H200 GPUs in-country and publishes docs for **OpenAI-compatible model endpoints** with API keys and NPR per-second billing. But access is a request form, and its rates are "being finalised" ([corevalley.ai](https://corevalley.ai/); [docs.corevalley.ai](https://docs.corevalley.ai/)). Its footnotes are dated 13 October 2026, so it may be the only real "since October" arrival. **YetiCloud.ai** launched on 3 July 2026 as "Nepal's first GPU-as-a-Service". It reportedly has about 12 GPUs, no rate card, and a speaker at the launch conceded "It's probably also not 99.99 uptime" ([TechSansar](https://techsansar.com/computing/yeticloud-ai-nepal-sovereign-compute/); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/datahub-launches-ai-cloud-in-nepal-in-partnership-with-hosted-ai/)). Both clouds sell infrastructure, not Nepali speech. For Boli they are possible hosts for keeping data in Nepal, not competitors.

The newcomer most likely to compete directly is **Awaj AI**. It lists voice cloning, multilingual TTS and STT, and audio agents, and says its next version is "almost here" after a "long private beta". Signup is a "Notify me" waitlist with no docs or pricing ([awaj.ai](https://awaj.ai/)). A search snippet describes its "native Nepali Voice APIs". A related Ekghanti "AwajAI" page returned a 404, so we could not confirm who is behind Awaj or where it is based ([ekghanti.com.np/awaj-ai](https://ekghanti.com.np/awaj-ai/)). On the institutional side, **Bhashini and Kathmandu University** signed an MoU on 6 June 2026 for a "voice first" Nepali platform covering STT, TTS, MT and conversational AI ([ANI](https://aninews.in/news/national/general-news/digital-india-bhashini-signs-mou-with-kathmandu-university-for-co-creating-national-digital-infrastructure-for-voice-first-language-translation-platform-for-nepal20260607173939/)). By September the reports described only "discussions", with no pilot, API or dataset ([APAC News Network](https://apacnewsnetwork.com/2026/09/bhashini-and-kathmandu-university-discuss-language-ai-for-nepal/)). The government's **Syuchatar Sovereign AI Compute Centre** is in the May 2026 budget, but has no amount, operator or timeline ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/columns/2026/06/07/nepal-s-ai-budget-the-right-instinct-and-the-trap-inside-it)). Nepal Telecom and Ncell run customer chatbots and make device deals, and neither offers developer AI ([Ratopati](https://english.ratopati.com/story/87286/samsung-nepal-and-ncell-partner-to-expand-ai-access-and-drive-4g-transition)).

Two limits apply to all of these scores. First, we read public pages only, with no signups and no rendered demos. So "self-serve" means the page says so, and the WiseAI app, Koili's key step and the Swar Playground's login rules are unverified. Second, local press beyond what search surfaced may cover launches we missed.

## For Boli, the self-serve slot is empty and TTS is the widest gap

Nepali STT is no longer scarce. Google Chirp, Azure, ElevenLabs Scribe, AssemblyAI, Deepgram and Sarvam all sell it self-serve, at roughly **USD 0.003-0.016 per minute**. Those figures come from secondary price roundups that disagree with each other ([Future AGI STT guide 2026](https://futureagi.substack.com/p/speech-to-text-apis-in-2026-benchmarks); [convertaudiototext pricing 2026](https://convertaudiototext.com/blog/speech-to-text-api-pricing-2026)). Boli cannot win STT on availability. It has to win on Nepal-standard and code-mixed accuracy, and on billing. That is a real opening: Sarvam serves only `ne-IN`, and a 2026 practitioner guide reports that production Nepali voice apps still mostly call foreign APIs, with a TTS voice that "still has a clearly foreign accent" ([Praxium Labs](https://praxiumlabs.com/blog/nepali-voice-assistant/)).

Nepali TTS is the thinnest category anywhere. **Sarvam has none**. Azure has two neural voices (`ne-NP-HemkalaNeural`, `ne-NP-SagarNeural`), ElevenLabs supports Nepali only in v3 and v4, and Google's Gemini-TTS lists `ne-NP` as Preview ([Azure language support](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/language-support?tabs=tts); [ElevenLabs models](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models); [Gemini-TTS docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/docs/gemini-tts)). In Nepal, the only self-serve TTS accounts are Koili's half-built one and TingTing's TTS buried inside campaigns. A Nepali TTS API with real self-serve keys, several voices, streaming and cloning would beat Sarvam on Nepali and beat every Nepal company on access. Transliteration is a second, cheaper opening, because no Nepal company sells it hosted. An OpenAI-compatible Nepali chat endpoint is a third, but a thin one. The local models are tiny, so the value lies in routing and Nepali tuning, not in owning a frontier model.

The threats are specific. Ampixa or Naamche could put an API key in front of their playgrounds. Awaj AI could come out of its waitlist. Bhashini x KU could eventually publish a free government-backed Nepali pipeline. CoreValley could start hosting the labs' open models next to its OpenAI-compatible endpoints. Boli's model choices also need an update. NepaliConformer, the October dossier's streaming ASR pick, is no longer public. Rani is the new open streaming candidate, but its licence terms are unread. The share-alike and GPL licences on Swar, Kala and sanoTTS need a legal read before any of them sits behind a paid API.

## Conclusion

The question has changed since October. Then it was whether Nepal had good open models. It now does, from two labs, and they keep improving. Now the question is who turns those models into a product a developer can sign up for in five minutes. No one has, and the onboarding pieces exist only in separate places: TingTing's free credits, Koili's key, ScaLabs' OpenAI-compatible endpoint, TalkC's published prices and CoreValley's NPR billing. Boli's opening is to combine them into one Nepali platform with real keys and real prices, lead with the TTS that Sarvam lacks, and treat the open-model labs as suppliers before they become rivals.

The best moat is not model quality. Labs that publish their weights will keep closing that gap, and Bhashini could make basic Nepali speech free. Boli's lasting advantages are the things those players are not set up to do: `ne-NP` and code-mixed tuning, NPR billing, hosting in Nepal on clouds like CoreValley, and Sarvam-compatible request shapes, so a developer can switch with a URL change. Being first matters too. Awaj AI's waitlist shows someone else sees the same slot.

## Addendum (23 November 2026): SAGEA

SAGEA (https://www.sagea.space/) is a Nepal AI lab selling enterprise products: Helios KYC (in production at Khalti), Document AI, a coding agent, custom training (Foundry) and VORA multilingual speech. It has an API platform at https://platform.sagea.space/, but it is login-only with credentials issued by support, and there is no public pricing or playground. It also publishes open weights on Hugging Face (`sagea-ai`), including SAGE 2.6 Celer Low, a 5B model whose card claims native optimisation for Nepali and Hindi in Devanagari (no licence tag). On the checklist it scores about 2.5, level with the runners-up: closer to Sarvam's shape (own models plus an API platform) than most, but not self-serve. For Boli's chat track, Celer Low is worth adding to the local model test once its licence is clear.
