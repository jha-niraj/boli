Open any group chat in Kathmandu and count the scripts. A message in English, a reply in Nepali written with Latin letters, a third that switches halfway through a sentence. Devanagari shows up too, but far less than you might expect from a country whose official language is written in it.

Boli is built for that chat, not for the textbook.

## Three ways to write one thought

The same question, three ways people actually send it:

| How it is written | Example |
| --- | --- |
| English | My exam is over, what should I do now? |
| Romanized Nepali | mero exam sakiyo, aba k garne? |
| Mixed | Exam sakiyo, now k garne? |

And Romanized Nepali has no fixed spelling. *cha*, *xa* and *chha* are the same word. So are *malai* and *mlai*. Any test of a model for Nepal has to include those variants, because people type all of them.

## Why models answer in Devanagari

Ask a small open model a question in Romanized Nepali and it will often understand you and then answer in Devanagari. Sometimes it answers an English question in Nepali. Sometimes it drifts into Hindi words.

The reason is the data the model learned from: most Nepali text on the web that models train on is in Devanagari, and Hindi is close by. When the model recognises Nepali, Devanagari is the likely continuation. That is fine for a newspaper. It is wrong for a person who just typed in Latin letters on a phone.

In our tests, the failures fell into a few named kinds:

- **Language drift** - a Romanized question, an English answer.
- **Script drift** - a Romanized question, a Devanagari answer.
- **Hindi bleed** - Hindi words in what should be Nepali.
- **Misreading** - a word read as something else entirely.

## How Boli answers in kind

The fix that worked is plain and explicit. <Fact name="kura" /> sends a system prompt that maps each way of writing to the same way of answering: English to English, Romanized Nepali to Romanized Nepali, a mix to the same mix in Latin letters, and Devanagari only when you write in Devanagari.

That mapping line is load-bearing. A shorter prompt without it made the model answer English questions in Devanagari again.

With it, the model we run (<Fact name="model" />) stayed in the user's script in 6 of 8 test prompts. The other small open model we tested managed about 3 of 8, and often answered in Hindi. You can send the same prompt with your own API calls - the [model page](/models/boli-chat) shows it in full.

## What is still not right

Six of eight is not eight of eight. The model still slips on grammar, still uses a Hindi word now and then, and still sometimes answers an exam question in English. Those gaps are the brief for the next step: fine-tuning an open model on real Nepali, written the way people write it.

Until then, Boli tells you what it is: a small open model that reads how Nepal types and does its best to answer the same way. [Try it in Kura](app:/kura) with a message you would actually send.
