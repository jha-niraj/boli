Boli document intelligence, chapter three - 25 November 2026

Building Kagaj

The step-by-step plan for Kagaj (कागज, paper), Boli's document product: what we build, in what order, why each step exists, and the traps inside each one. The model comes first, then the platform. No code to read; every step says what it produces and how we will know it is done.

Chapter one: what document intelligence is Chapter two: how a document model is trained

How this chapter differs. Chapters one and two were the map: every option, every model, how others did it. This one is the route: the choices are made, the order is fixed, and each step has a check. Where something is still your decision, it says so and the question waits for section 5.

1. What Kagaj is, and what version one is not

The promise: upload a Nepali or English document (a PDF, a scan or a phone photo) and get back its text in the right reading order, its tables as tables, and the fields you ask for as clean data. On Boli's own open models, from the app or the API.

In version oneNot in version one
Printed Nepali (Unicode and old Preeti-font documents), English, and pages that mix themHandwriting. It is a different, much harder job and needs its own data.
Digital PDFs, scanned PDFs, phone photos of one pageChecking whether a document is genuine (fraud, tampering)
Two outputs: Read (the whole page as typed blocks) and Extract (fields into a JSON shape you choose)Long books. A cap of about 20 pages a file at first.
Bills, invoices, notices, letters, certificates, statements, formsTranslation and summaries as separate products. Once the text is clean, Kura or the API already do those.

That last row is the most important planning fact in this chapter. Writing is already solved on Boli; reading is not. Every hard step below is about getting the characters, the digits and the order right.

2. Where we stand, in our own numbers

On 24 November we ran Stage 0 on 39 real, public Nepal government documents (IRD, MoF, NEB, NTA, DoHS and others: 11 digital PDFs, 16 scans, 5 photos, 7 degraded copies). Mac M5, Gemma 4 E4B with its vision add-on, Tesseract 5 with Nepali. The full report is ml/docintel/testset/REPORT.md. Six numbers carry the whole plan:

Digits, vision only
71.7%
right on digital PDFs. Most misses are dropped digits; 1 in 13 is a real misread.
Reading, Tesseract vs vision
0.21 vs 0.29
character error rate (lower is better). The old OCR engine reads characters better.
Dates on scans
13 / 16
with the hybrid. Vision alone: 5 of 16. Tesseract alone: 12 of 16.
Speed per page
19 s vs 1 s
vision transcription vs Tesseract, on the Mac. One run looped for 92 s.
PDF text layers
10 of 11
digital PDFs whose own text was wrong: Preeti gibberish (3) or broken Word exports (7).
Scans with junk OCR
7 of 16
scanners added a Latin-letter "text layer" to a Devanagari page.

Three lessons fall out, and each becomes a step:

  1. No single reader is good enough. The vision model understands the page but misreads and drops digits. Tesseract reads characters well but knows nothing about layout. So version one is a hybrid that uses each for what it is good at (step M3).
  2. A PDF's own text cannot be trusted. Most people would assume "digital PDF = exact text". On Nepal government PDFs that is false 10 times out of 11. So every file goes through a router first (step M2).
  3. Digits fail silently. ०.१९ became ०.११; the VAT Act year २०५२ became २०७२; a crore-sized amount changed in the middle. The text still looks perfect. So every number the model writes must be checked against a second reader before we return it (step M3), and our scoring measures digits on their own (step M4).

3. The whole build on one page

Two tracks. The model track decides how well Kagaj reads; the platform track decides how people use it. The model track goes first because its answers change the platform: how long a page takes decides whether the screens can wait or must run a background job, and what the reader returns decides what the screens show.

The order is the plan. M6 is optional on purpose: if the hybrid passes the gate, fine-tuning moves to "later, to make it faster and cheaper" instead of "now, to make it work".

The model track, step by step

Every step below has the same four parts: what we do, what it produces, the nuance (the part that is easy to get wrong), and done when (the check that lets us move on).

M1. Freeze the output shape

1 to 2 daysdecides everything after it

What we do. Write down, exactly, what Kagaj returns. Two outputs, always in this order:

  • Read: the page as a list of typed blocks in reading order: title, heading, paragraph, list, table, key_value, stamp, signature, footer. Each block has its text and a rough position on the page (a box). Tables keep rows, columns, header and total rows.
  • Extract: named fields from those blocks into a JSON shape. Either one of our templates (bill, invoice, notice, certificate) or a shape the user writes.

What it produces. A one-page spec and three hand-made example outputs. The gold set (M4), the scorer and the screens (P4) all read this spec.

The nuance: copy, never correct. The model writes what is printed, exactly: Nepali digits stay Nepali, a Bikram Sambat date stays BS, रु. १,२४०.०० stays as printed. Our code, not the model, then adds the normalised values next to the printed ones: ASCII digits, the AD date, the amount in paisa. Why? A model asked to convert a BS date to AD will sometimes do the arithmetic wrong and you cannot tell. Code is either right every time or wrong every time, and a test catches it once.

{ "field": "due_date",
  "printed": "२०८२/०८/१५",      // what the page says, copied
  "value":   "2082-08-15",      // our code: digits converted
  "ad":      "2025-12-01",      // our code: BS calendar to AD
  "verified": true,             // M3: the OCR reader agrees on every digit
  "page": 1, "box": [412, 188, 140, 22] }

Done when you have read the spec and the three examples and said "yes, this is what a customer gets".

M2. The router: which reader for this file

3 to 5 daysfixes 3 traps we measured

What we do. A small piece of plain code that every page passes through first. It looks at the file and picks the route. No model, under a second.

Green boxes trust the PDF's text; red boxes do not. A route is chosen per page, because one PDF can mix a typed letter with a scanned attachment.

What it produces. For each page: the route, the reason, and (for text routes) the cleaned text. This reason is stored and shown in the admin, so when a page reads badly we know which door it came through.

The nuance. The broken Word export is the sneaky one. Its text looks like Nepali (ववभाग for विभाग, सूचर्ना for सूचना), so a script check passes it. Only comparing it with an independent read catches it: we measured its error rate at 15 to 24%, worse than Tesseract on the same page. But its digits were right in both hand checks, so the router keeps them as a second opinion for M3's digit check. Also: those PDFs store ASCII 2053 while the page shows २०५३, because the font draws ASCII digits as Nepali ones. Comparing digits by value, not by character, is what makes this a match instead of an error.

Done when the router picks the right route on all 39 test documents, and we can say why for each one.

M3. The hybrid reader (what ships first)

1 to 2 weeksalready proven on one bill

What we do. Three readers work on the same page, each for what it does best, and a referee decides:

ReaderGood atBad atIts job in Kagaj
Tesseract (OCR, about 1 s)Exact characters and digitsLayout, tables, reading order, stampsThe witness for every character and digit
Gemma 4 E4B with vision (about 19 s)Layout, reading order, tables, what a number meansNepali digits; can drop lines or invent textWrites the typed blocks, given Tesseract's text as a hint
The PDF's own text (when the router trusts it)Exact text, freeOften broken (M2)Replaces Tesseract as the witness when available
The referee (our code)Checks every number the model wrote against the witness. Marks fields verified or not. Never invents a fix it cannot justify.

How the digit check works. Take every number in the model's output. Look for the same value in the witness text, in the same region of the page (the box). Three outcomes:

  • Found: the field is verified: true.
  • Not found, but the witness has a number of the same shape in that box (the model wrote ०.११, OCR has ०.१९ in the same place): use the witness's value, mark it corrected_by_ocr, and lower the confidence.
  • Nothing close: keep the model's value but mark it verified: false. The screen shows it in amber and the API says so. We never pretend.

The nuance: a fluent mistake is worse than a garbled one. If Tesseract writes nonsense, everyone can see it is wrong. If the vision model writes a perfect-looking Nepali sentence that is not on the page, nobody notices. This is why the referee stays in production even after a fine-tuned model ships: a second reader is the cheapest insurance there is. Second nuance: loops. One page repeated a sentence until it hit the 3,000-token cap. The reader stops a page whose output starts repeating and retries it once at a lower token budget.

Done when on the test set the hybrid gets dates and amounts at least as right as Tesseract alone, writes no invented fields, and every unverified field is marked.

M4. The gold set and the scorer

2 to 3 weeks, mostly people's timethe asset that outlives every model

What we do. Grow the 39 test documents into about 300 pages with a hand-checked answer for each, then freeze it with a date. Mix: digital PDFs, scans, phone photos (crumpled, angled, shadowed), Preeti documents, tables, bills, letters, notices. Two Nepali readers check every answer; where they disagree, a third decides.

What it produces. A folder that never changes after the freeze, and one script that scores any reader on it and prints one row. Every decision from here on is "which row is better".

CERCharacter error rate: how many characters must change to turn the output into the answer, divided by the answer's length. 0.05 means 5 wrong in 100.
Digit accuracyOnly the digits, in reading order, compared by value (४ = 4). Scored on its own because a wrong digit costs more than a wrong letter.
Field accuracyOf the fields we asked for, how many came back exactly right after normalising. The number a customer feels.
TEDSTree edit distance similarity for tables: did the rows, columns and merged cells come out the same shape?
Script slipsCharacters in a script that should not be there (Bengali inside a Nepali table: PaddleOCR-VL did this). Target: zero.
Invented fieldsA value with no source on the page. Target: zero. This is the one that loses customers.

The nuance: leakage. If two pages of the same gazette go one to training and one to the gold set, the model has seen that layout and that font, and the score lies. Split by document and by publisher, never by page. Second nuance: privacy. Only public documents and documents people give us with written consent; names and numbers of private people are blacked out before anyone labels them.

Done when 300 pages are frozen, two readers signed off, and the scorer prints a row for Tesseract, vision-only and the hybrid.

M5. The bake-off

about 1 week

What we do. Run every candidate reader over the frozen gold set and fill one table: the hybrid with Gemma 4 E4B, the hybrid with a bigger open vision model, Qwen3-VL at 4B and 8B, PaddleOCR-VL, Tesseract alone. Sarvam goes in as an outside reference only, so we know the gap.

The nuance: the licence register. Before a model goes in the table, we write down the licence of the exact checkpoint. The trap from chapter two: one model family's 7B is Apache 2.0 while its 3B is research-only, and a fine-tune inherits the licence. A model we cannot sell is not a candidate, however well it scores. Second nuance: score the model quantised the way we would run it (4-bit or 8-bit), not the full-size version from the paper.

Done when the table is full and one row is the clear winner on field accuracy with zero invented fields.

M6. Fine-tune, only if the hybrid stalls

6 to 10 weeksoptional at first

When. If the best hybrid misses the gate (M7) and the misses are the model's knowledge (it does not know Nepali digit shapes), not its eyesight. Chapter two covers how in full; in one line: render tens of thousands of synthetic Nepali pages in many fonts including Preeti, add real pages with consent, label them with an open teacher plus human checks, and train a LoRA adapter on a rented GPU for a few hundred dollars a run.

The nuance. Even when the hybrid passes, fine-tuning comes back later for a different reason: speed and cost. A specialist that reads well on its own can drop the second reader on easy pages and answer in 3 to 5 seconds instead of 20.

Done when a checkpoint beats the hybrid on the gold set and stops improving between runs.

M7. The gate

a dayno gate, no platform

What we do. Agree the pass marks before seeing the results, so the bar does not drift to whatever we got. A starting proposal, yours to change:

Check on the gold setProposed bar
Invented fields0
Script slips0
Field accuracy, clean digital and scanned pages95% or more
Field accuracy, phone photos85% or more
Digit accuracyat least Tesseract's
Unverified fields that are actually wrong, but not marked0

Done when the winner passes every row. Then the reader is fixed, and the platform track starts.

4. Where Kagaj runs, and why not on Cloudflare

Everything Boli serves today runs on Cloudflare Workers. Kagaj's reader cannot, for three reasons you should know:

  • Workers cannot run programs. Tesseract and the PDF renderer (pdftoppm) are native programs. A Worker runs JavaScript only.
  • The model needs a GPU. On the Mac a page takes about 19 seconds; a GPU brings that to a few seconds. Chat is the same story, which is why boli-chat already runs on its own server.
  • Pages are big. A 200 dpi page image is a few megabytes; a 20-page PDF is too much to pass around inside one request.

So the reader lives in a reader service next to the chat model on the GPU host: one small private web service that takes a page and returns blocks. The Cloudflare side (the app, the API and the jobs Worker) stores files, keeps the records, runs the step-by-step job and talks to the reader service. This is the same split as chat: the app on Cloudflare, the model on our own box.

The cost question this raises. The Mac is fine for building and for the gold set. Selling Kagaj needs an always-on GPU host. That is the same host the chat model needs to leave the Mac, so it is one decision, not two. It is the first question in section 5.

The platform track, step by step

P1. How the pieces connect

Black text on light boxes. Blue runs on Cloudflare; yellow runs on our GPU host. The page in your browser asks the database every few seconds how far the job has got.

Why a background job. Boli's rule (AGENTS.md section 5): anything that can take more than about 30 seconds, must survive a closed tab, or must retry is a long job. A 10-page scan is minutes. So the app answers at once with "received", the jobs Worker does the work page by page, and the screen shows progress. This is the same machinery Jawaf already uses to read websites.

P2. The tables

Three new tables, each carrying the workspace id like everything else in Boli:

TableOne row perHolds
kagaj_documentuploaded filename, the R2 key of the file, page count, status (queued, reading, done, failed, partly done), credits charged, who uploaded it, when it will be deleted
kagaj_pagepage of a fileroute and reason (M2), status, attempts, seconds taken, the typed blocks (Read), the R2 key of the page image
kagaj_extractextraction requestthe template or JSON shape asked for, the fields with printed value, normalised value, verified flag and page box

The nuance. The file itself never goes in the database, only its R2 key (Boli's rule: store keys, not URLs). And "partly done" is a real status: if page 7 of 10 fails three times, you still get the other nine, with page 7 clearly marked.

P3. One job, step by step

  1. Upload. The browser sends the file straight to R2 with a short-lived signed link (big files never pass through the app). The app checks the size, type and page count, and that the workspace has credits for every page.
  2. Start. A kagaj_document row is written and a Workflow starts. The app returns at once.
  3. Split. The reader service counts pages and renders each to an image at 200 dpi, stored in R2. One kagaj_page row each.
  4. Read each page, as its own step: route (M2), read (M3), store the blocks. A Workflow step retries on its own (up to 3 times, waiting longer each time) and is idempotent: running it twice gives the same row, never two.
  5. Extract, if asked: one more step over all pages' blocks with the chosen shape.
  6. Charge and finish. Credits are charged for the pages actually read, once, in the same write that marks the document done. A failed page is not charged.

The nuance: charging. Charge at the end, per page read, never up front for the whole file. If we charged at upload and the job died, the person pays for nothing; if we charged per step, a retry could charge twice. One charge at the finish, keyed to the document, cannot double.

P4. The screens

Kagaj gets its place on the product rail next to Kura, API and Jawaf, with its own sidebar:

  • Read: drop a file, watch pages tick through. Then a split view: the page image on the left, the blocks on the right. Hover a block and its box lights up on the page. Copy as Markdown, HTML or JSON.
  • Extract: pick a template (bill, invoice, notice, certificate) or describe the fields. Each field shows its printed value, the clean value, and a quiet mark: verified, corrected by the second reader, or not verified (amber).
  • Files: everything read in this workspace, searchable, with when each will be deleted.
  • Code: the same request as an API call in five languages, like Kura's Code tab.

The nuance. The verified mark is the product. Anyone can show text next to a picture. Showing which numbers a person must double-check, and which they can trust, is what makes a business rely on it.

P5. The API

POST /v1/documents          # a file, or a URL; options: extract template or JSON schema
  -> 202 { "id": "doc_...", "status": "queued", "pages": 4 }

GET  /v1/documents/doc_...  # poll until done
  -> { "status": "done", "pages": [ { "blocks": [...], "route": "image" } ],
       "fields": { ... }, "usage": { "pages": 4, "credits": ... } }

The nuance. This is not the OpenAI format: OpenAI has no document endpoint, and forcing a 10-page job into a chat call would time out. Same keys, same credits, a different shape. Webhooks (we call your server when it is done) come after polling works. The price per page is yours to set (section 5); it must cover the GPU time of about 20 seconds a page today, less after M6.

P6. Privacy

Documents are the most sensitive thing Boli will ever hold: citizenship cards, bank statements, salary letters. So, by default:

  • Deleted after a set time (proposal: 30 days), the file, page images and results, with a "delete now" button.
  • Never used for training unless the workspace owner turns on a clearly worded opt-in, and even then names and ID numbers are blacked out first.
  • Never logged. Logs carry ids and timings, never text from a page.
  • Reachable only by the workspace, checked on every read like every other Boli table.

P7. Kagaj inside Jawaf

Today Jawaf reads a business's uploaded files with Sarvam, as an allowed stopgap. Once Kagaj passes its gate, Jawaf's file step calls Kagaj instead. Two wins: Boli is open models end to end again, and Jawaf gets the verified marks for prices and fees, which is exactly where a wrong digit hurts most.

5. What I will ask you before we plan

Read, come back with your questions, then I will ask these with the picker, one group at a time. They are here so you can think about them first. Nothing below is decided.

The model

  • The GPU host: which provider and budget, and whether chat moves to it at the same time.
  • The gate's pass marks (M7): keep the proposal, or stricter for money fields?
  • The first three document types to optimise for (bills? IRD notices? bank statements?).
  • Who checks the gold set: paid Nepali readers, people you know, or you?
  • Bake-off candidates: anything to add or remove?

The platform

  • Price per page, and whether Read and Extract cost different amounts.
  • The limits: pages per file, file size, files per day on the free credits.
  • Retention: 30 days, 7 days, or the workspace chooses?
  • Opt-in training: offer it at launch, or later?
  • Kagaj on the public site: a landing page like Kura and Jawaf from day one, or after the pilot?
